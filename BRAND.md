# The Helping Sun — Brand

## The idea in one line

**The sun comes up whether you asked it to or not.** Help that shows up
without needing to be requested well.

## Name

**The Helping Sun** — Ian Sun's own name, which means the brand can never be
separated from the person. That's a feature. People trust a person, not a logo,
especially in this category. Never shorten it to "THS" in public.

## Positioning

Not a wellness brand. Not a coach. Not a clinic.

> Peer support from someone who was on the other side of it, for people who
> are too ashamed, too tired, or too proud to ask properly.

The competitive landscape is full of polished, serene, credentialed wellness.
The gap is **unpolished and honest** — someone who names the unopened mail pile,
the 3 p.m. shower, the $12 balance on a Tuesday. Specificity is the entire moat.

## Voice

| Do | Don't |
| --- | --- |
| Short, plain, concrete sentences | Therapy-speak, "holding space", "journey" |
| Name the actual thing (the mail pile, the ghosted friend) | Abstractions like "challenges" and "wellness" |
| Admit limits out loud | Imply clinical authority |
| "I" and "you" | "We at The Helping Sun believe" |
| Scripts people can literally say | Affirmations |

**Test for any sentence:** would it sound insane said out loud to a friend at
a kitchen table at 1 a.m.? If yes, cut it.

## Type

- **Newsreader** — display. An editorial serif with real warmth; the italic
  carries the emotional lines.
- **IBM Plex Sans** — body. Grounded and humane without being the default Inter.
- **IBM Plex Mono** — labels, dates, timestamps. This is the important one: the
  mono gives the whole thing a *field-log* quality rather than a spa quality.
  It's what keeps the design honest instead of soothing.

## The signature asset: the Sunrise Log

Ian photographs the sunrise on the mornings that are hardest to start. The
timestamps in the Drive folder tell the real story — `064330`, `064355`,
`064338` on three consecutive mornings.

**The photo isn't the point. The proof is.** Each image is evidence that he got
up. This is simultaneously:

1. The visual identity (free, infinite, unmistakably his)
2. The daily content engine (one photo + one line = a post, even on a bad day)
3. The thesis of the entire business, stated without a single platitude

Protect this. Never use stock photography. Never use an AI-generated sunrise.
The moment the images stop being real, the brand is just another wellness page.

## Logo

A simple sun with rays over a horizon line (inline SVG in `site/index.html`).
Works at 16px. Don't commission anything more elaborate — the photographs are
the identity; the mark is just a bookmark.

## Marks

Two marks, one system. Both live in `brand/`.

**The roundel** (`mark-photo-ring-512.png`) — a circular crop of the Nov 4
sunrise over Walton Lighthouse, taken from `20261104_064349.jpg`. The sun sits
at 40% height and the horizon at 62%, so the gold band survives all the way
down to 32px. Use it as the profile picture on every platform. It is the most
personal mark available, because it isn't a drawing of a sunrise — it is the
actual morning.

**The line mark** — the inline SVG sun in `site/index.html`. Use it in the site
header, the favicon, and anywhere the roundel would be too busy or needs to be
one flat colour (stamps, print, embroidery).

Sizes supplied: 1024, 512, 256 (plain) and 1024, 512 (with the gold ring). The
ring exists so the dark foreground doesn't dissolve into a white feed
background. On dark backgrounds either works.

### The vector mark

`brand/logo-mark.svg` — a circular badge: gold sun above a clean horizon, with
the Walton Lighthouse in silhouette on the headland. Drawn from the Nov 4
photograph's own composition, so the mark and the roundel are the same scene
in two registers.

Variants: `logo-mark.svg` (light), `logo-mark-dark.svg` (for dark surfaces),
`logo-mark-mono.svg` (single colour via `currentColor` — for stamps, embroidery,
one-colour print, the business card). PNG exports from 1024 down to 32.

It degrades correctly: below about 48px the lighthouse disappears and the mark
reads as sun-over-horizon. That is intended. Don't add detail to "fix" it.

**When to use which**

| Use | Mark |
| --- | --- |
| Social profile pictures | the photo roundel — it's the real morning |
| Favicon, app icon | `logo-mark.svg` |
| Site header | the inline line mark in `index.html` |
| Print, stamps, the card, merch | `logo-mark-mono.svg` |
