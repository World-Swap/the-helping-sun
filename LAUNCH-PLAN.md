# The Helping Sun — Launch Plan

Read `SAFETY-AND-LEGAL.md` first. This document assumes it.

---

## 1. Structure — you already have the entity

You have an LLC and a business bank account, which skips the whole first stage
of this plan. Three things still need attention.

### File "The Helping Sun" as a DBA under the LLC

Unless the LLC is already named The Helping Sun, you need a **fictitious
business name** so you can trade, invoice and take payment under that name.
In California that's filed with the **county**, not the state: Santa Cruz
County Clerk, roughly $50–60, plus publication in a local paper for four
consecutive weeks.

Without it, a client agreement signed "The Helping Sun" is a name that doesn't
legally exist, and banks will refuse cheques made out to it.

### Tell your insurer what you're now doing

This is the one that actually matters. If the LLC carries any policy, it was
written for whatever the LLC did before. **Peer support and coaching are a
different risk class and almost certainly aren't covered.**

Call the broker and describe the work in the unprotected language from
`SAFETY-AND-LEGAL.md` §1 — peer support, accountability, navigation. Never
"counselling". Then add general liability and professional liability (E&O).
Expect roughly $40–70/month.

An insurer that was never told about the activity can decline the claim, which
is the worst possible moment to discover the gap.

### Keep the books separable

Same LLC and same bank account is fine, but tag every Helping Sun transaction
so the line of business can be reported — and separated later if you ever spin
it out or move the charitable side to a nonprofit. A second free checking
account under the same LLC makes this trivial and costs nothing.

### Still true from before

⚠️ **Do not solicit public donations as a for-profit LLC.** Taking money "for
the fund" without charitable registration creates real problems in California.
Either stay services-only, or use a fiscal sponsor — a community foundation
hosts you under their 501(c)(3) for ~5–10%, which lets you accept
tax-deductible donations without forming anything.

If the Helping Hand fund ever becomes the main event, that's the moment to
revisit a 501(c)(3). Not before.

## 2. The first 90 days

### Days 1–14 — Foundation (nothing public)

- [x] LLC and business bank account — already in place
- [ ] "The Helping Sun" DBA filed with Santa Cruz County Clerk
- [ ] Insurer told about the change of activity
- [x] `hello@thehelpingsun.com` — live on Zoho, 10/10 authenticated
- [x] Site deployed to `thehelpingsun.com` — live, auto-deploys on push
- [ ] Crisis protocol **printed** (county number is in it: 1-800-952-2335)
- [ ] Insurance quotes requested
- [x] Instagram `@the_helping_sun` and Facebook `thehelpingsun` claimed
- [ ] Claim the same name on TikTok, YouTube and Threads (park them, don't post)
- [ ] **Your own support booked** — therapist or peer group
- [ ] Ask one person to be your co-pilot

### Days 15–30 — Ten quiet conversations

**Do not launch publicly yet.** Instead: ten free *First Light* calls with
friends, family, and people they refer. No posts, no logo reveal.

This is market research wearing kind clothes. After each call write down:
*What did they actually need? What did they ask for? What did they refuse?*

You will discover the real business here, and it will not be what you expected.
Most people in this category find the demand is for **practical navigation**
(forms, calls, appointments) far more than for conversation.

**The one metric that matters: people who reached out.** Not followers.

### Days 31–60 — Start talking

- Newsletter live (**Golden Hour**, weekly) on Beehiiv or Ghost
- **One** social platform, properly — not five, badly
- Publish 3×/week (see `CONTENT.md`)
- Order **the card** (see below)

### Days 61–90 — First offers

- Open **Sunrise Circle** with six people
- First paid 1:1 slots
- First B2B conversation (see §3)
- Review: is the capacity cap holding? Are you okay?

## 3. How this makes money

Ordered by how soon each is realistic. The free tier is permanent and is the
whole point — it is also the top of the funnel, and those two facts aren't in
conflict as long as you never pressure anyone upward.

| | Offer | Price | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Free | First Light, Warm Line, newsletter | $0 | Permanent. Never gated. |
| 1 | **Sunrise Circle** membership | $15–25/mo | Six people, weekly hour. Low effort, recurring, and the most *useful* thing on the list. |
| 2 | **1:1 accountability** | $75–150/session, or $250–400/month | Non-clinical. Needs insurance and a client agreement. |
| 3 | **Workshops** | $25–50/seat | "Money Shame", "Getting Through Winter", "How To Ask For Help". Record once, run repeatedly. |
| 4 | **Talks** | $500–2,500 | ⭐ The real money. Trade schools, unions, small employers, churches, recovery orgs — all have budgets for a speaker on mental health and financial stress, and your lived experience is exactly what gets booked. One talk a month beats 300 subscribers. |
| 5 | **Prints of the sunrise photos** | $25–60 | You already have the inventory. A stated % to the Helping Hand fund. Low volume, high brand value. |
| 6 | Grants / sponsorship | — | Only after nonprofit or fiscal sponsorship. |

**Don't:** ad revenue (needs scale you won't have for years), affiliate
supplements or "mindset" products (destroys the trust that is your only asset),
anything requiring you to be available 24/7.

## 4. Two cheap ideas with outsized returns

**The Helping Sun card.** A physical business card: the name, the sun mark,
your number, and one line —

> *Text me when it's bad. 2 p.m. or 2 a.m.*

~$30 for 500. Hand them to friends, family, bartenders, barbers, trade-school
instructors, the guy at the hardware store. This solves the exact problem you
described: it reaches people who will never fill in a web form, and it gives
*other* people something to pass along when they don't know what to say.

**The Sunrise List.** A spreadsheet: who you're checking on, what's going on,
when you last spoke, when to reach out next. Unglamorous, and it *is* the
business. The product you're actually selling is that someone remembered.

## 5. What success looks like at 6 months

Not followers. Not revenue, yet.

- 25+ people have reached out
- 6 people in the Circle, showing up weekly
- One person who says you changed something — in writing, with permission
- You are still well. The cap held. You took time off.

If the last one isn't true, nothing else counts.
