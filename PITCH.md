## Two things I checked in the terms first

**You are eligible.** Participating countries include the United States, "the
50 states and District of Columbia". California qualifies. You must be
physically in the US when you apply.

**The scoring is published — and there are two separate scorecards.** They are
easy to conflate, and writing to the wrong one wastes your only entry.

**Your 60-second video is scored only on this:**

| Weight | Criterion |
|---|---|
| **30%** | Clarity, structure and persuasive quality |
| **30%** | Evidence of execution and real-world progress |
| 20% | Customer problem and value |
| 15% | Business quality and credible next steps |
| 5% | Learning and resourcefulness |

**The $100,000 Grand Prize uses a different one**, applied live at the Grand
Finale: clarity 35%, evidence 25%, customer problem 20%, **business model and
potential 10%**, **responsible use of the Grand Prize 10%**.

Those last two exist only at the Finale. Do not try to cram them into 60
seconds.

**And this is the part worth internalising:** the terms say that at the
Finale, *"Earlier Video scores, Application material, Course records and
community activity do not carry forward."* The final is a clean slate,
decided entirely on the live pitch and the judges' questions.

So the video has exactly one job: **get you into the 180.** It does not need
to win the hundred thousand, and trying to make it do both is how it ends up
doing neither.

Two further things in the terms: 60% of the video score is clarity plus
evidence, so be plainly spoken and full of things that exist. And course
completion, community participation and follower count do **not** affect
eligibility or scoring — nobody is counting your followers. They also say AI
may be used in judging given the volume, which is another reason specific,
checkable facts beat atmosphere.

## Which track to enter

There are five — App Builder, **E-commerce**, **Creator**, Education,
Services — with **36 awards in each**. Track choice is the one strategic
decision you make.

**Enter E-commerce.** It is the truest description: you sell a physical
product through a storefront. "Creator" is the alternative, but it invites
comparison with people whose business is an audience, and your audience is
small — you would be judged on your weakest axis. "Services" is the worst
fit: your support work is free, so as a *business* it has no model, and a
judge will see that in ten seconds.

The mission still carries the pitch. It just enters as the reason the
e-commerce exists.

## The script

**148 words**, which lands at about 59 seconds read at a measured pace — a
deliberate second of headroom, because "60 seconds or less" is a limit
and a rushed 61 is worse than a calm 57. Time yourself once. If you run long,
cut "in Santa Cruz, California", not anything after it.

> **[0:00]** I'm Ian Sun, in Santa Cruz, California. Eighteen months ago I was
> depressed, broke, and too embarrassed to tell anyone.
>
> **[0:09]** I started photographing sunrises because I needed a reason to get
> out of bed. I've taken a hundred and forty-seven since. They're all at
> thehelpingsun.com.
>
> **[0:20]** Here's the business. Forty-five are listed as prints. A partner
> prints and ships every order — no stock, nothing upfront. The margin funds
> one thing: free peer support for people carrying what I was carrying. Their
> mental health, their health, their money.
>
> **[0:36]** I'm not a therapist. I'm the person who answers — who'll sit with
> you while you open the mail you've been avoiding.
>
> **[0:47]** It's free because nobody should have to work out whether they can
> afford to ask.
>
> **[0:50]** Five thousand dollars covers a year of running costs and puts the
> next hundred photographs on sale. A hundred thousand means I answer
> full-time.

### Why it is built this way

- **The first line is the hook and it is true.** Most pitches open with a
  market size. Yours opens with a person. Judges watching their four
  hundredth video will remember it.
- **Every claim is checkable.** 147 photographs, 45 listed, a live site, a
  named mechanism. That is the 30% for evidence of execution.
- **The model is explained in one breath** — no stock, no upfront cost,
  margin funds the support. That is the 15% for business quality.
- **The ask is specific and proportionate.** $5,000 as a year of runway, not
  "to grow". $100,000 as the thing that changes what you can do. Vague asks
  read as not having thought about it.
- **You never say "passionate", "journey" or "impact".** Say what happened.

### If you have made any sales

Add one sentence after "...pay nothing upfront":

> "I've sold [N] so far."

Even one is worth saying — it moves the business from plausible to proven,
and that is the highest-weighted thing you can show. **If you have not sold
any, say nothing about sales.** Do not imply it. Judges at this volume are
well practised at spotting the gap between what was said and what was shown.

## What to film

It is a 60-second video. The terms say camera quality is not scored, so do
not spend the five days on production.

**Best version:** you, outside, at the rivermouth or on West Cliff, just after
sunrise or near sunset. Phone on something steady, framed from the chest up,
light on your face, the water behind you. One take, no cuts. It costs you one
morning and it looks like the thing you are describing.

**Acceptable version:** you talking straight to the phone indoors, window
light on your face, plain wall behind. Clear beats pretty.

**Do not** make a slideshow of your photographs with voiceover. Judges score
*you* on clarity and persuasiveness, and a disembodied voice over pictures
reads as hiding. Your face saying this is the pitch.

**If you can manage one cut**, hold the last line to camera and let the final
two seconds be one photograph — the Boardwalk under the crimson sky. Nothing
more.

### Before you record

- Learn the shape, not the words. Reciting sounds recited.
- Say the numbers exactly: 147, 45, eighteen months.
- Slow down more than feels right. Nervous people speed up, and clarity is
  30%.
- Record it three times and send the third. The first is stiff, the second is
  over-corrected.

## Two things that could cost you the money after you win

**Every claim gets checked.** The terms require "truthful information and
proportionate evidence of any material claim checked during verification". So
147 photographs and 45 listed must still be true on the day you submit, and
you must be able to show it. They are, and you can — the site and the shop
both prove it in one click. This is also the hard reason not to mention sales
you have not made.

**The money is paid "to a verified account used for the recipient's
Business".** Not a personal account. SMG Payments LLC is dormant, and The
Helping Sun has no banking of its own. **Sort this before you submit**, not
after you win: decide which entity is "the Business" on the application, and
make sure it has an account that can receive US dollars in its name. Getting
this wrong is how people pass judging and then fail verification.

Also worth knowing before you plan around the number: awards are paid
**gross**. HelpBnk sends exactly $5,000 and tops up nothing — withholding,
receiving-bank charges and your own taxes come out of it. Payment is
instructed by 29 January 2027, expected to land by 31 January.

If you would rather not be listed publicly, you can object to publication, or
ask them to publish less, **when applying or later, without giving a
reason** — and the terms say it does not affect entry or judging. They
ordinarily publish surname, country, track, award and business name.
