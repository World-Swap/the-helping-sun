# The Launch Post + 30 Days of Content

Ready to post. Edit anything that doesn't sound like you, but don't smooth it
out — the roughness is the asset.

**Assumption:** year one stays free (First Light and the Warm Line at no cost,
Sunrise Circle opening later). Nothing below sells anything. If you decide to
price things, the only change needed is in Week 4, Day 26.

**Cadence:** three real posts a week, plus a Sunrise Log photo on the days
between. That's a month of content and it survives a bad week, which is the
only test that matters.

**Before every post that touches crisis:** put `988` in the caption.

Send this **individually**, never as a group message. This is the one that
actually starts the business.

> Hey — I've started something I wanted you to hear from me first.
>
> It's called The Helping Sun. It's support for people going through hard
> stuff, mental health, health, money. I'm doing it because I went through all
> of it alone and I don't want that for anyone else.

**Then put your phone down for two hours. Genuinely.** Set a timer. Watching
the notifications roll in on launch day is how people end up feeling worse on
the best day of the project.

## Day 3 — The Scripts: the creditor call ⭐

Carousel. This format will outperform everything else you make.

> **Slide 1**
> What to say when you can't pay a bill.
> Word for word. Save this.
>
> **Slide 2**
> The word you need is **HARDSHIP**.
> Almost every lender has a hardship program.
> Almost none of them will mention it first.
> You have to say the word.
>
> **Slide 3**
> Call the number on the statement. When someone picks up:
> *"I'm having trouble making my payments. I want to stay current. What
> hardship or forbearance options do you have?"*
>
> **Slide 4**
> Then ask these four, in order:
> 1. *"Will this show on my credit report?"*
> 2. *"Does interest keep adding up during the program?"*
> 3. *"Can you email me that in writing?"*
> 4. *"Can I get your name and a reference number?"*
>
> **Slide 5**
> Things you do NOT have to do:
> Explain your whole life. Apologize. Cry (it's fine if you do).
> Agree to anything on the first call.
> *"I need to think about it"* is a complete sentence.
>
> **Slide 6**
> If the call is the part you can't do:
> I'll sit on the line with you while you make it.
> That's a real thing I do, and it's free.
> thehelpingsun.com

**Caption:** "I avoided a phone call for eleven weeks once. The call took nine
minutes. The eleven weeks cost me more than the debt did. Save this for when
you need it, or send it to someone who does."

## Day 5 — The Hard Stuff: the mail pile

Photo of actual mail on an actual counter. Yours. Unstaged.

> There were four unopened envelopes on my counter for eleven weeks.
>
> I knew one was from the DMV. I knew the pile was getting worse every day I
> didn't touch it. I knew that opening them would take about twenty minutes.
>
> I still couldn't do it.
>
> This is the part people don't understand about depression. It isn't that you
> don't know what to do. You know exactly what to do. You can see it. You just
> cannot make your hand reach out.
>
> What finally worked wasn't discipline. It was having someone on the phone
> while I opened them. Not helping. Just there. Three of the four were nothing.
>
> If you have a pile, you're not lazy and you're not stupid. You need a second
> person in the room. That's all. That's the whole trick.

## Day 8 — One Small Thing: light before noon

Sunrise photo.

> One small thing, and it's free:
>
> **Ten to twenty minutes of outdoor light, before noon.**
>
> Not a sun lamp. Not through a window. Outside, even if it's overcast —
> an overcast morning still beats your brightest indoor room by a mile.
>
> This is not a cure. Nobody is telling you a walk fixes depression. It
> measurably helps with sleep and mood, it costs nothing, and it asks almost
> nothing from you on a day when you have almost nothing.
>
> You don't have to walk. You can stand in a doorway with a coffee.
>
> That's the whole post. Go outside for ten minutes before noon.

## Day 10 — The Scripts: telling a doctor you've been avoiding it ⭐

> **Slide 1**
> What to say to a doctor when you've been putting it off for years.
>
> **Slide 2**
> Open with this. It changes the entire appointment:
> *"I've been putting this off for about two years. I'm embarrassed about it.
> I need help getting started."*
>
> **Slide 3**
> Why it works: it tells them you need extra patience, it gets the shame out of
> the room in one sentence, and good doctors hear it constantly. You are not
> the first person this week.
>
> **Slide 4**
> Three things to do beforehand:
> Write down your top 3 concerns. Hand them the paper. Ask for the longest
> appointment slot they have.
>
> **Slide 5**
> If your head is the problem, the magic phrase with insurance is:
> *"I need a behavioral health referral."*
> If you don't have insurance: community health centers charge on a sliding
> scale. findahealthcenter.hrsa.gov
>
> **Slide 6**
> The best question to end with:
> *"If you were me, what would you do first?"*

**Caption:** "Three years. That's how long I avoided an appointment about
something that turned out to be minor and fixable. The avoiding was worse than
the thing. Every single time, the avoiding is worse than the thing."

## Day 12 — The Hard Stuff: looking fine

Face to camera, 45 seconds, no script.

> I want to talk about how good some of us are at looking fine.
>
> I held jobs. I showed up. I made jokes. I asked people how their weekend was
> and I meant it. And I was, at the same time, in a place I couldn't describe
> to anyone.
>
> Here's the part that took me years to understand: nobody was ignoring me.
> They genuinely could not tell. I had made sure of that. I'd spent a decade
> getting good at it.
>
> So if you're waiting for someone to notice — I need to tell you kindly that
> they might not. Not because they don't love you. Because you're good at this.
>
> Which means the first move is yours. That's unfair. It's also true.
>
> If making that move to someone who knows you is too much, make it to me
> instead. I'm a stranger, and sometimes a stranger is easier. That's allowed.

## Day 15 — The Scripts: the friend you ghosted ⭐

> **Slide 1**
> What to say to someone you disappeared on.
>
> **Slide 2**
> Send exactly this:

> You think they're angry. Usually they're relieved. Occasionally they're hurt,
> which is fair, and survivable.
>
> **Slide 5**
> The longer you wait the harder it gets, and the story you're telling yourself
> about how angry they are is being written by the illness, not by them.
>
> **Slide 6**
> Send it today. Don't draft it for a week.
> One message. Sixty seconds.

**Caption:** "I lost friendships to this that I didn't need to lose. Not to a

## Day 17 — One Small Thing: 211

> Most people have never heard of this, and it's one of the most useful numbers
> in the country.
>
> **Dial 211.**
>
> Free, 24/7, every county. A real person who knows what help exists where you
> live: rent assistance, utility bills, food, transport to appointments,
> childcare.
>
> Not a charity you have to prove yourself to. An operator with a directory.
>
> I didn't know it existed during the years I most needed it. Nobody tells you.
> So I'm telling you.
>
> **211.** Save it in your phone right now, before you need it. Saving it now
> is the whole point, because the day you need it is the day you can't look
> anything up.

## Day 22 — The Scripts: when someone says they want to die ⭐

The most important post you will make. Pin it.

> **Slide 1**
> What to say when someone tells you they want to die.
> Most people freeze. Here's what to do.
>
> **Slide 2**
> **Ask directly.**
> *"Are you thinking about killing yourself?"*
> Say the words. Asking plainly does not plant the idea — this is one of the
> most studied things in the field. Avoiding it is what loses the thread.
>
> **Slide 3**
> **Then say this:**
> *"I'm glad you told me."*
> *"I'm not going anywhere."*
> Not advice. Not perspective. Not "think of your family." Just stay.
>
> **Slide 4**
> **Do not promise secrecy.**
> *"If I think you're about to die, I'm going to get other people involved. I'm
> telling you that now so it's not a betrayal later."*
>
> **Slide 5**
> **If there's a plan, a method at hand, or a timeline:**
> Stay with them and call **988** together, or **911** if it's immediate.
> Don't leave to go find help. Help comes to you.
>
> **Slide 6**
> **Tomorrow, follow up. In writing.**
> The follow-up is the part almost everyone skips, and it's the part that
> proves the first conversation was real.
>
> **Slide 7**
> **988** — call or text, 24/7
> **741741** — text HOME
> Screenshot this. You will need it one day for someone you love.

**Caption:** "I've been on both ends of this conversation. The thing that
helped wasn't anything clever anyone said. It was that they didn't leave and
they came back the next day. 988."

# Rules for posting (read before you start)

- **Never post from inside a bad day.** Draft it, hold it, publish when you're
  on the other side. The one exception is a short calm "today's hard, I'm okay,
  back tomorrow", which is honest and good. Raw distress is not content. It's
  you needing support, and you deserve the real kind.

- **Never use anyone's story without written permission**, even anonymised.
  Ask again before you publish, because people say yes in a good week and
  regret it in a bad one.

- **Never screenshot a DM.** Not once. The entire business is built on people
  believing you won't.

- **If a post goes big, expect replies you can't handle.** Pin a comment with
  988, 741741 and 211, and say plainly what your capacity is. Being honest
  about limits is part of the service.

- **Batch the filming.** Shoot three videos in one sitting on a decent day.
  Your future self on a bad Tuesday will be grateful.
