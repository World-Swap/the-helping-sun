# Email replies

Nobody who writes to this business should feel ignored — that is the whole
point of it. But a reply should never leave an opening either.

Every template below works the same whether the sender is genuine or not:
warm, brief, and it sends all money through the shop. A real buyer follows the
link. A scammer goes quiet, because there is no overpayment to run.

---

## 1. Purchase enquiry — the one to use by default

Use this for any "I'd like to buy your work" message, including vague ones.
It doesn't accuse anyone of anything and it doesn't need you to decide first.

**Subject:** `Re: your message`

```
Hi [name],

Thanks for getting in touch, and for the kind words about the work.

All of my photographs are available as prints — paper, canvas, metal and a
few other things besides — through my shop here:

https://fineartamerica.com/profiles/ian-sun

You can choose any image and any size or format, and they take care of the
printing and delivery straight to you. All my sales go through there, so it's
the fastest way to get something on your wall.

If there's a particular photograph you're after and you can't find it listed,
tell me which one and I'll get it up there for you.

Best,
Ian

Ian Sun · The Helping Sun · Santa Cruz, California
thehelpingsun.com
```

**Why this is safe.** "All my sales go through there" closes the direct-payment
door politely, in advance, without calling anyone a fraud. You never quote a
price, never take a payment, never ship anything yourself.

---

## 2. When they name an actual photograph

If somebody mentions a real title — *First Light*, *The Wheel*, *Fly Agaric* —
they have genuinely looked. Warmer, same destination.

```
Hi [name],

Thank you — [TITLE] is one of mine too. It was taken [WHERE/WHEN].

It's on my shop here, where you can pick the size and the material:

https://fineartamerica.com/profiles/ian-sun

They print and ship it directly. If you want a size that isn't listed, say so
and I'll add it.

And in case it's of interest: prints pay for the free peer support side of
what I do, so buying one keeps that going for somebody else.

Best,
Ian
```

---

## 3. If they push back — check, own shipper, overpayment

The moment any of these appear, the conversation is over. One line, no
explanation, no argument.

```
Thanks — I only sell through my shop, which handles payment and delivery.
The link is in my last message. All the best.
```

Then stop replying. Don't explain why, don't accuse. They move on to the next
artist.

---

## 4. Somebody reaching out for support

Different inbox entirely in spirit. Answer like a person, fast.

```
Hi [name],

Thanks for writing. That took something, and I'm glad you did.

I'll come back to you properly [today/tomorrow]. In the meantime, if things
get bad: 988, call or text, any hour. 1-800-952-2335 for Santa Cruz County.

You don't have to have anything organised before we talk.

— Ian
```

---

## Never put in a reply

- A price, or an offer to invoice
- Your phone number or address
- Agreement to any shipper the buyer nominates
- A high-resolution file "to check quality"
- Any detail about who else has bought, or how new the business is

## Never accept

- **A check.** For any amount, for any reason.
- **An overpayment.** No genuine buyer overpays and asks for change.
- **A request to forward money to a shipper.** This is the whole scam.
- **Shipping before funds have settled** — settled, not merely showing.

## The structural answer

Selling only through the platform means the money never touches you, so the
overpayment fraud has nothing to attach to. That is a better reason to use it
than convenience.

Keep direct sales for people you can see — a local buyer, a market, somebody
who walks up. Anyone emailing out of nowhere goes to the shop.
