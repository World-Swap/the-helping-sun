# Safety, Scope, and Legal Guardrails

> Not legal advice. Everything here should be confirmed with a California
> attorney and an insurance broker before you take your first client. But these
> are the specific things to confirm, which is most of the work.

This is the most important document in the repository. A support business that
hurts someone — or collapses because its founder burned out — helps nobody.

---

## 1. Words you cannot use

In California, these titles are protected by the Board of Behavioral Sciences.
Using them without a licence is a misdemeanour, regardless of intent:

**therapist · psychotherapist · counselor · social worker · psychologist ·
marriage and family therapist**

Also avoid implying clinical activity: *treatment, diagnosis, patient, clinical,
cure, therapy session, prescribe, prognosis*.

**Use instead:** peer support, companionship, navigation, accountability,
someone to sit with you, help finding a professional.

The site copy already follows this. Keep it that way in every caption, bio and
reply. The riskiest place is a casual DM, not the website.

## 2. A credential worth getting

California has run a **Medi-Cal Peer Support Specialist Certification** since
2022. It's built precisely for people with lived experience of mental health
and substance use challenges. It is realistic to obtain, it teaches exactly the
skills this business needs (boundaries, crisis escalation, ethics), and it
converts "guy on the internet" into "certified peer support specialist."

Look up the county behavioural health department's approved training entity for
Santa Cruz County. This is the single highest-leverage credibility investment
available, and it is cheap compared to any degree.

## 3. Money and health advice — the second trap

Debt settlement, credit repair, and "proraters" are heavily regulated in
California (and federally under the Credit Repair Organizations Act). Never:

- negotiate with a creditor *on someone's behalf*
- take a fee connected to a debt outcome
- recommend specific investments, or tell someone to stop paying a debt

What you *can* do, which is most of the value anyway: sit beside them while
**they** make the call, tell them the word "hardship" exists, and refer to an
**NFCC-accredited nonprofit credit counsellor** (free or near-free). Same shape
for health: never advise, always accompany and refer.

## 4. Crisis protocol — write it before you need it

You will get a message from someone who is suicidal. Decide now, not then.

**Print this. Keep it by the desk. Memorise the order.**

1. **Stay in the conversation.** Do not go silent to go find a resource.
2. **Ask directly:** *"Are you thinking about killing yourself?"* Asking plainly
   does not plant the idea; avoiding it loses the thread.
3. **If there is immediate danger** (a plan, the means at hand, a timeline) —
   stay with them and get emergency services involved.

   > **Santa Cruz County Behavioral Health Access & Crisis Line**
   > ## 1-800-952-2335
   > 24/7, every day. The same number reaches the **Mobile Crisis Response
   > Team**, which has operated around the clock since December 2024 — follow
   > the prompts for mobile crisis. Hearing impaired: dial **711**.
   > Call **911** instead if there is immediate physical danger.

   **Walk-in crisis services**, Monday–Friday 8:00 a.m.–4:00 p.m.:
   1400 Emeline Ave, Bldg K, Santa Cruz · 1430 Freedom Blvd, Ste F, Watsonville

   Verify this number once a year. County lines change.
4. **If there is no immediate danger** — **988**, call or text. Stay on with
   them while they connect if they'll let you.
5. **Never** promise secrecy. Say up front: *"If I think you're about to die,
   I'm going to get other people involved."* Put that in the welcome message so
   it is never a surprise.
6. **Follow up the next day, in writing.** Always.
7. **Write down what happened** the same day, factually, and store it securely.

**Adults only, at least for year one.** Anyone under 18 brings mandated
reporting, parental consent, and background-check obligations you are not set up
for. Say so publicly and refer minors to their school counsellor or 988.

## 5. Insurance

Before the first conversation with anyone outside your family:

- **General liability** + **professional liability (E&O)** sized for coaching
  and peer support. Expect roughly $40–70/month. Hiscox, Next, and
  coach-specific programs all write this.
- Tell the broker exactly what you do, in the unprotected language above. If
  you describe it as "counselling", you may be sold the wrong policy or denied
  a claim later.

## 6. Privacy

You are **not** a HIPAA covered entity (you're not a provider or a health plan),
which means you get none of HIPAA's protections but hold the same devastating
information. Act as though you were covered:

- One password manager. Two-factor on everything.
- Notes in one encrypted place, not in your phone's Notes app, not in email.
- **Never screenshot a DM.** Not for a post, not for a friend, not ever.
- Written consent before sharing any story, even anonymised. "Can I use this?"
  in writing, every time. People say yes in a good week and regret it in a bad
  one — so ask again before you publish.
- A plain-language privacy policy on the site before the form goes live.
- Decide a deletion policy and honour it.

## 7. Protecting the founder — the part nobody writes down

You are describing a business built on your own worst years, run by someone who
is still inside some of them. The most common failure mode for this kind of
venture is not lawsuits. It's the founder going under while carrying everyone
else.

**Hard rules, set now while you're thinking clearly:**

- **Capacity cap.** A fixed maximum of active people — start at five. Write the
  number down. When you're full, you're full; a waitlist is kinder than a
  half-presence.
- **Published hours.** "I answer between 9 a.m. and 9 p.m." A 24/7 promise is a
  promise to fail. 988 covers the hours you don't.
- **Your own support.** Somewhere you are the one being helped — not someone
  you're also helping. Non-negotiable. See `YOUR-SUPPORT.md` for options,
  starting with NAMI Santa Cruz County, which is free.
- **A co-pilot.** One other person who knows the work and checks on you inside
  it. Launching entirely alone is the single biggest structural risk here.
  `YOUR-SUPPORT.md` has the words to ask someone.
- **A written "when I'm not okay" protocol.** Decide in advance: what pauses,
  what message goes out, who gets told. Put the message in a draft today so you
  never have to write it on a bad day.
- **Never counsel someone through the exact thing you're actively drowning in.**
  Refer it out. That's not failure; it's the job done properly.
