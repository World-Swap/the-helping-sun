# Your own support, and your co-pilot

Two different things that have been sitting on the checklist unexplained.
Here's what each one actually is and how to set it up.

Neither is a formality. You are building a business out of years you're partly
still inside, and the usual way these end isn't lawsuits — it's the founder
going under while carrying everyone else.

---

# 1. Your own support

**Somewhere you are the one being helped.**

That's the whole definition. A place where you are not the strong one, not the
one who answers, not the one holding it together for somebody else. Someone
whose job in that room is *you*.

**The test:** if you find yourself supporting other people there, it isn't your
support. It's more work wearing a different hat. You will be tempted — you're
good at it and it's more comfortable than being the one who needs something.
Notice that and resist it.

## Options, cheapest and fastest first

### Free, this week — NAMI Santa Cruz County

Free, peer-led **Connection Recovery** groups for adults who've experienced
mental health symptoms. **No diagnosis required.** Drop-in, no application,
no fee.

### Low cost — Open Path Collective

A nonprofit network of therapists offering reduced rates.

- **$65 one-time lifetime membership** (not recurring)
- **$40–70 per 50-minute session** after that
- **$30** with pre-licensed therapists and interns

openpathcollective.org. The cheapest route to an actual therapist of your own.

### If you have insurance

Call the number on your card and say exactly this:

> *"I need a behavioral health referral."*

That phrase moves things. Ask for someone taking new patients, and ask how
many sessions are covered.

### County services

**1-800-952-2335** is Santa Cruz County Behavioral Health's line for *access
to services*, not only crisis. You can call it to ask what you're eligible for.

## A different thing worth having: consultation

Separate from therapy. **One hour a month with an experienced peer support
specialist or a clinician**, to talk through the work itself — a conversation
that went badly, someone you're worried about, where your line should be.

Standard practice in this field. It protects your clients and it stops you
carrying every hard call alone. Ask NAMI or the county whether they know a
certified peer support specialist who takes consultation.

# 2. Your co-pilot

**One named person who knows this exists and checks on you about it.**

Not a therapist. Not a business partner. Not someone you're supporting. Just
one person who knows what you've taken on and has agreed to keep an eye on how
*you* are doing inside it.

## What they actually do

- **Check in with you every week or two.** Fifteen minutes. "How's it going,
  and how are *you* going?" — two separate questions.
- **Hold a copy of your crisis protocol** (`SAFETY-AND-LEGAL.md` §4), so if
  something happens they already know what's involved.
- **Be callable** when you're not okay. Not to fix it. To know.
- **Notice if you go quiet.** You went quiet for years and nobody noticed.
  This is the person whose job it is to notice.
- **Know what to pause** if you need to stop for a while.

## Who

Someone steady who you'd believe if they said "you don't sound right." A
friend, a sibling, a cousin, a former colleague, someone from a group. They
don't need any professional background at all.

**Not** someone you're currently supporting. That inverts the relationship and
hurts you both.

## The script

You built a business because you couldn't ask for help. So here are the words.
Send it or say it, roughly as written.

> **"Can I ask you something kind of big?**
>
> **I've started doing this thing — The Helping Sun. Supporting people who are
> going through it with their mental health, their health, their money. It's
> real now, there's a website and everything.**
>
> **The thing is, I'm doing it because I went through all that mostly alone,
> and I don't want to do this one alone too. I need one person who isn't
> someone I'm helping — who just knows it exists and checks on me about it.**
>
> **It's not a job. About fifteen minutes every couple of weeks, asking how
> I'm actually doing. And being someone I can call if I'm not doing well.**
>
> **Would you be that person for me?"**

### Shorter, by text

> **"Hey — I've started something called The Helping Sun, supporting people
> who are struggling. I need one person who keeps an eye on how *I'm* doing
> while I do it. 15 mins every couple of weeks and being someone I can call
> on a bad day. Would you be up for that?"**

### If they say no

Ask someone else. A no is about their capacity, not your worth — they may be
carrying things you can't see. Ask the next person. You only need one.

# 3. The "when I'm not okay" protocol

Write this **now**, while you're thinking clearly, and give a copy to your
co-pilot. Fill in the blanks and keep it with the printed crisis protocol.

### What pauses, in order

1. ☐ New intakes — no new people while I'm under
2. ☐ Posting and the newsletter
3. ☐ The Sunrise Circle (co-pilot tells the group)
4. ☐ Everything, including the Warm Line

### Who gets told

- Co-pilot: ______________________  phone: ______________
- Anyone I'm actively supporting: they get the message below, nothing more

### Pre-written — so I never have to write this on a bad day

**To people I'm supporting:**

> *Hi — I need to step back for a little while. It's nothing you've done and
> nothing to do with you. I'll be back in touch when I'm able. In the
> meantime: 988 any hour, call or text, and 1-800-952-2335 for Santa Cruz
> County. Please use them — that's what they're for.*

**To post publicly:**

> *Taking a short break. I'm okay and I'll be back. If today is hard for you:
> 988, call or text, any hour.*

### For my co-pilot

If I go quiet for more than ______ days without saying why, please:

1. Call me. Not text — call.
2. If you can't reach me, contact ______________________
3. If you're worried I'm in danger: **988**, or **911** if it's immediate
