Who's behind this

I'm Ian Sun. I've spent most of my life on the other side of this page — depressed, isolated, broke, and too embarrassed to say any of it out loud. I continue to try and get through it without anyone reaching back. I don't recommend that route.

The Helping Sun exists because I kept waiting for someone to notice, and nobody did. Not because people are cruel — because I got very good at looking fine. If that's you, I already know what you're doing, and you don't have to explain it to me.

I'm not a therapist. I'm the person who answers. I'll sit with you while you open the mail you've been avoiding, or make the call you can't make, or just say the true thing out loud for the first time.

— Ian Sun, founder

Nobody should have to be articulate to get help.

What we talk about

The three things people are most ashamed to bring up.

None of these are character flaws. All of them are easier with a second person in the room. Here's where I'd start — real first steps, not affirmations.

Mental health

The 3 a.m. ones

Depression, anxiety, the isolation that builds its own walls, the thoughts you haven't said to anyone.

  • Get 10–20 minutes of outdoor light before noon. It is not a cure. It measurably helps, and it's free.
  • Tell one person one true sentence. Not the whole thing. One sentence.
  • Ask a doctor for a PHQ-9. It's nine questions, takes two minutes, and gives you language.
  • With insurance, the phrase is I need a behavioral health referral.

Physical health

The body keeps the receipts

The appointment you've rescheduled twice. The pain you've decided to live with. The sleep that stopped working.

  • One fixed wake-up time, seven days. Sleep repair starts at the morning end, not the night end.
  • A ten-minute walk, not a gym membership you'll quit in March.
  • No insurance? Community health centers charge on a sliding scale — findahealthcenter.hrsa.gov.
  • Booking it is the hard part. Try I've been putting this off and I need help getting started.

Financial health

The unopened mail pile

Debt, overdrafts, the envelopes you stopped opening. Money shame is the quietest and the heaviest of the three.

  • Open all of it in one sitting, with someone on the phone. The pile is almost always smaller than the dread.
  • The word that unlocks things is hardship. Nearly every lender has a program and won't mention it first.
  • Call 211 for rent, utilities, and food assistance in your county. Free, 24/7.
  • Free credit counseling exists through NFCC-accredited nonprofits. Never pay a "debt settlement" company up front.

Four ways in. All of them start with you saying one thing.

01

A free 30-minute call. No agenda, no form to fill out first, no need to have it organized in your head. You can cry, ramble, or sit quietly. I've done all three.

Tell me as little as you want.

Three words is enough. "Things are bad" is enough. You don't owe me an explanation, a diagnosis, or a tidy story. I answer every one of these myself.

Being straight with you

What this is, and what it isn't.

What it is

  • Peer support from someone who has been through it
  • Company while you do the hard administrative parts
  • Help finding real professionals, and sitting with you while you call them
  • Someone who follows up when you go quiet

What it isn't

  • Therapy, counseling, or any kind of clinical treatment
  • Medical advice or a substitute for seeing a doctor
  • Financial, legal, or debt-settlement advice
  • A crisis line or emergency service — that's 988 and 911

If what you need is a therapist, I will help you find one and stay on the line while you book the appointment. Getting you to the right person is a win, not a handoff.
